GUARDIAN MOTION MODULE - STAGE 4 PART A
How existing fall detectors work, and what we take from them
(7 Oct 2026)

==============================================================================
1. THE FOUR KINDS OF FALL DETECTOR
==============================================================================

A. Threshold rules (Kangas 2008, Bourke, Chen)
- Compute |a| (the sum vector). If it crosses an impact threshold, maybe after
  a free-fall dip, and the posture afterwards looks like lying, call it a fall.
- Cheap and easy to explain. Kangas got 97-98% sensitivity at the waist and
  head, but the wrist "was not applicable" with the same rules.
- On real falls they break. Bagala 2012 tested 13 of them on 29 real falls:
  mean sensitivity 57%, specificity 83%. Bourke3 had claimed 100/100 on
  simulated falls and got 82.8/96.7 on real ones.
- They miss low-impact falls (onto a bed, against a wall, onto the knees),
  and every extra condition is one more thing that can fail.

B. Hand-made features + classic ML (Aziz 2017, Marques & Moreno 2023)
- Cut a window around the event, compute features, train SVM / kNN / trees.
- Aziz: SVM 96% sensitivity and specificity at the waist, young people only.
- Marques & Moreno built WEDA-FALL itself. Their final detector used only
  accelerometer max, min, mean and variance per axis, a 9 s window, 50 Hz
  and 3-NN. In a pilot of 238 hours over 5 users it caught 25 falls and
  missed 3. The watch asked the user to confirm each alarm and added the
  answers to its training set, which cut false alarms over time.

C. Deep learning on the raw signal (XAI-Fall 2022 and others)
- LSTM / CNN learn their own features. XAI-Fall: 92.54% on UMAFall.
- In our Stage 2 the CNN and CNN-LSTM lost to the tree models: 60-70% of
  falls caught at 95% specificity against 96-98%, and 28-40 false alarms
  per hour. Not worth it on our data size.

D. Commercial watches (Apple Watch)
- After a hard fall it taps the wrist, sounds an alarm and shows an alert.
  If the person stays still for about a minute, it counts down 30 s and
  then calls emergency services. On by default for users 55 and over.
- Apple says itself that it "cannot detect all falls" and that high-impact
  activity can trigger it.
- So even Apple uses two stages: an impact, then a check on what happens
  after it, with the user able to cancel.

==============================================================================
2. WHAT THE CROSS-DATASET PAPERS SAY
==============================================================================

- Silva, Casilari & Garcia-Bermudez 2024: 13 algorithms over 17 datasets.
  Every one got worse when tested on a dataset it was not built on. Waist
  sensors only.
- Fula & Moreno 2024 (wrist, closest to us): UP-Fall + WEDA-FALL + UMAFall,
  all resampled to 18 Hz, 2 s windows, 78 features cut to 26-28, SVM and
  ANN. Trained on all three combined: recall 90.6%, specificity 96.9%,
  AUC 0.989. They also trained on one dataset and tested on the others:
  WEDA-FALL was the best for learning what daily activity looks like.
  Scaling the data per dataset gave mixed results.
- Our Stage 3.5 agrees: the WEDA-trained model travelled best, and a
  threshold tuned on one dataset did not carry over to the others.

==============================================================================
3. LESSONS FOR GUARDIAN
==============================================================================

1. A threshold set on one device does not suit another (Bagala; our
   Stage 3.5 test). The real band will need its own.
2. Low-impact falls beat impact-based detectors. Fusion needs other paths
   (stillness, heart rate), which is already the plan.
3. What happens after the impact matters. Apple waits about a minute. Our
   model looks only 1.5 s past the impact; stillness comes later and only
   feeds fusion.
4. Longer windows are common: Marques used 9 s, ours is 3.5 s.
5. Letting the user cancel an alarm (Apple's countdown, Marques' feedback)
   is the usual way false alarms are kept down. That belongs in the
   decision manager, not the model.
6. Heart rate helps at the wrist (Nho 2020). Fusion again.

==============================================================================
4. OUR ALGORITHM IN SIMPLE STEPS (as it stands after Stage 3.5)
==============================================================================

1. Read the wrist accelerometer (g) and gyroscope (deg/s) at 50 Hz, in
   2.5 s windows.
2. Make every device look alike: clip at +/-8 g, squeeze the signal down to
   20 Hz and back up to 50 Hz.
3. Gate: if |a| goes above 2.25 g, something hit hard. If not, the score
   is 0 and nothing else runs.
4. Cut 3.5 s around the first crossing: 2 s before it, 1.5 s after.
5. Compute 18 numbers from that piece: peak, lowest and range of |a|, the
   free-fall dip and how long before the peak it came, samples above 2 g,
   jerk (peak and average), gyroscope peak, total and before/after
   averages, how jumpy |a| was before and after, the after/before energy
   ratio, forearm tilt change and final tilt, and whether gyro exists.
6. XGBoost (depth 4, 100 trees, trained on WEDA-FALL) turns them into an
   impact probability from 0 to 1.
7. The stream reports that probability and holds it for 60 s, fading.
   Stillness over the next 10 s is reported separately for fusion.
8. The 0.886 threshold is only for testing. Fusion makes the alert call.
